# The Domain Loop — engagement architecture for a year-long name-memory practice

*Independent design research, conducted for Nomen. Where the source is the user's memory-science
brief it is marked **[brief]**; everything else is separately sourced and linked at the bottom.*

---

## 0. The problem this document solves

The memory science tells us what to make the user *do*. It says nothing about how to make a person
still be doing it in month eleven. That is the harder problem, and it has its own literature.

The constraint that makes this domain unusual: **the training material walks in off the street.**
Nomen cannot ship a curriculum of 10,000 items the way a language app can. Its content is
generated by the user's own social life, which is irregular, sometimes empty for a week, and
sometimes floods 30 names in one evening. Every engagement decision below falls out of that fact.

Three domain-specific hazards follow:

1. **Supply is bursty and involuntary.** A streak that requires meeting new people punishes a
   quiet week and is unsatisfiable during illness, travel, or introversion. *Any streak must be
   satisfiable with zero new people.*
2. **The reward is delayed and off-app.** The actual payoff — greeting someone correctly by name
   three weeks later — happens in the world, and the app is not there to see it. *The app must
   go and fetch that signal, because it is the only reinforcer strong enough to carry a year.*
3. **Review debt compounds silently.** A flood-week of 30 new people becomes a 40-card queue for
   the next fortnight, which is exactly the documented Anki-abandonment pattern. *Load must be
   capped at intake, not at review.*

## 1. What the engagement literature actually supports

### 1.1 Streaks work, and the mechanism is loss aversion — with a caveat
Duolingo's streak is the most-tested engagement mechanic in consumer software (reportedly ~600
experiments on that feature alone), and the reported pattern is consistent: day 0→7 is the
critical window, after which loss aversion takes over and churn drops sharply. The important
finding for us is the **streak freeze** result — giving users slack *increased* persistence
(reported ~3.3% D14 retention lift, ~10.5% more daily learners holding a streak), consistent with
academic work showing "emergency reserves" increase long-term goal persistence rather than
licensing slacking.

**Design consequence:** a streak, yes — but with earned freezes, an explicit rest day, and a
recovery path that never zeroes a year of real work over one missed Tuesday.

### 1.2 Gamification's effect is real but small, and it leans extrinsic
Meta-analytic estimates for gamified vs. non-gamified learning cluster around **g ≈ 0.26** —
positive, not transformative. More pointedly, gamification reliably raises *autonomy* and
*relatedness* perceptions but has **minimal effect on competence**, and it boosts *extrinsic*
motivation more than intrinsic. The overjustification risk is explicit in this literature:
bolting points onto an activity can crowd out the interest you were trying to build.

**Design consequence:** points-for-their-own-sake are the weakest available lever and the one with
a documented downside. Nomen's reward currency is *information about real competence*, not tokens.
Competence is the SDT need gamification is worst at feeding — so it is the one the domain design
must feed directly.

### 1.3 The Hooked/B=MAP critique is correct and worth designing around
B = MAP (motivation, ability, prompt) is a good trigger-design tool and a poor retention theory:
it says nothing about whether the behaviour produces satisfaction or resentment. The sharpest
formulation of the failure: a user grinding a meditation app out of streak-loss fear is not
building a meditation practice, they are building *app tolerance*. A variable-ratio reward
schedule can hold attention while actively degrading the intrinsic motive.

**Design consequence:** Nomen uses B=MAP for *prompt design only* (make the action tiny, prompt it
at the right moment) and SDT for *reward design*. Variable reward is permitted only in its
**informational** form — genuine surprise about your own memory — never as randomised loot.

### 1.4 Habits need ~2 months, not 21 days, and tolerate lapses
Lally's habit-formation work puts median automaticity at **~66 days** with a wide range
(18–254 days), and — crucially — found that a single missed day did not measurably harm
habit formation.

**Design consequence:** the Phase 1 encoding habit is budgeted at ~66+ days, not two weeks, and
the app says so. A miss is logged as a data point, not a moral event.

### 1.5 Timing beats frequency (JITAI)
Just-in-time adaptive intervention work is unambiguous that a prompt must land when the user is
both *vulnerable* (the behaviour is at risk) and *receptive* (they can act). A fixed 9am ping is
the weakest form of this.

**Design consequence:** Nomen's prompts are **context-triggered**, not clock-triggered, wherever
the user permits it: before a calendar event with attendees, on arrival at a recurring venue, at
the pre-sleep consolidation window, and the morning after a high-intake evening.

### 1.6 The review-debt abandonment spiral is the #1 killer in this exact category
The documented pattern for spaced-repetition tools: user adds cards without limit → misses a
weekend → returns to a wall of overdue reviews → the tool becomes a debt → abandonment. The
consensus fix is not willpower but **capping intake** and keeping the daily queue small.

**Design consequence:** Nomen caps *intake*, triages overdue items by real-world value, and has a
one-tap **Amnesty** that reschedules a backlog instead of demanding it be cleared. This is the
single most important engagement feature in the app and it is invisible when working.

## 2. The domain loop: five nested cycles

Each loop has a cue, an action sized to fit the moment, a reward drawn from real competence, and
an investment that makes the next turn of the loop better. Longer loops are fed by shorter ones.

```
  ┌─ SEASON  (6–12 wks) ─ phase advance, re-baseline, capability statement
  │  ┌─ WEEK  (5 min)   ─ report card, rescue at-risk names, unlock a drill
  │  │  ┌─ DAY (3–7 min)─ queue + field mission + pre-sleep consolidation
  │  │  │  ┌─ SESSION   ─ retrieve → feedback → next
  │  │  │  │  ┌─ MOMENT ─ the handshake: HEAR·SAY·LOOK·HOOK + 20s check
```

### Loop 1 — THE MOMENT (seconds, in the world)
The only loop that touches real life, and the one that carries the outcome.

- **Cue:** a handshake. Externally triggered; the app cannot prompt it. So the *protocol itself*
  must be the habit — trained by repetition until it fires without the phone.
- **Action:** four beats, then a 20-second self-check.
- **Reward:** immediate and self-evident — *you still have the name*. Nomen's job is to make that
  moment legible: a one-tap capture that says **"caught."**
- **Investment:** the capture becomes tonight's queue.
- **Engagement risk:** phone-in-hand during an introduction is socially costly. **Mitigation:**
  capture must survive a 4-second interaction — one field (the name) is enough to create a valid
  person; everything else can be filled in later from the "loose ends" queue. A voice-memo capture
  path exists for when typing is impossible.

### Loop 2 — THE SESSION (60–90 seconds)
- **Cue:** opening the app; the queue is pre-built so there is zero setup cost (B=MAP: ability).
- **Action:** retrieve, self-grade, get feedback.
- **Reward:** *competence made visible* — the SDT need gamification is worst at. After each
  correct retrieval the app shows the interval just cleared: "held for 8 days." That is a true
  statement about the user's brain, and it is a stronger reinforcer than a coin.
- **Investment:** grades sharpen the schedule.

### Loop 3 — THE DAY (3–7 minutes, three touchpoints)
- **Morning cue (context-aware):** "You have *Standup* at 10 with 4 people — 2 of their names are
  due." This is the JITAI move: vulnerable *and* receptive.
- **Field mission:** exactly one, tiny, real-world: *"Use two names out loud today."* Missions are
  the mechanism by which the app reaches into the world where the real reward lives.
- **Pre-sleep consolidation:** the day's new names, once, before bed **[brief: sleep protects the
  day's bindings]**. This slot is *also* the best habit anchor in the day — it is time-stable,
  low-competition, and already adjacent to an existing routine.
- **Reward:** the day closes with a *statement*, not a score: "3 names captured, 2 used aloud,
  6 retrievals held."

### Loop 4 — THE WEEK (5 minutes, Sunday)
- **Report card** on the four primary numbers, with `n` shown.
- **Rescue list:** the names about to decay past recovery. Framed as *rescue*, not backlog —
  loss-framing that is honest, because the decay is real **[brief: transmission deficit]**.
- **Unlock:** one new drill type per phase-appropriate week — a competence-gated reveal rather
  than a random reward.
- **Zeigarnik hook:** the week always ends with one open loop ("3 people you met once and never
  confirmed — want to fix that?"). Open loops are the cheapest legitimate return-trigger there is.

### Loop 5 — THE SEASON (6–12 weeks)
- **Re-baseline** on held-out assessment items — the anti-self-deception rail.
- **Phase advance** gated on measured criteria, not the calendar (see `03-year-program.md`).
- **Capability statement:** the app writes one honest paragraph about what the user can now do,
  and what remains out of reach. Identity reinforcement, earned rather than asserted.

## 3. The reward model: informational variable reward

Skinner-style random reward is available, effective, and wrong for this product — it degrades the
intrinsic motive the user needs to survive a year. But *variability* is genuinely motivating, and
this domain generates it for free, because **you do not know whether you still have a name until
you try to retrieve it.** Uncertainty is intrinsic to the material.

So Nomen's variable rewards are all *true facts revealed at unpredictable times*:

| Reward event | Trigger | Why it is honest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rescue** | You correctly retrieve an item that was scheduled to lapse | You genuinely just saved it |
| **Durability reveal** | An item clears a new personal-record interval | "Held for 94 days" is a measurement |
| **Field win** | You log a real moment where a name landed | The actual payoff, self-reported |
| **Streak-freeze earn** | Sustained adherence | Earned insurance, not a gift |
| **Phase unlock** | Gate criteria met | Competence-contingent |

There are **no** random bonuses, no loot, no leaderboard, and no comparison to other users
(there are no other users — the app is local-first). Relatedness is fed the only way this domain
allows: by making the user better at the social act itself, and by logging the moments it worked.

### The Moment Journal
One tap: *"It worked."* A timestamped line — who, where, how it felt. This is the app's most
important screen at month nine, when novelty is gone and the queue is a chore. It is the evidence
file that the year is paying off, in the user's own words. Reviewing it is a scheduled part of the
weekly loop.

## 4. Load balancing — the anti-abandonment engine

Concretely, in `domain/scheduler/loadBalancer.ts`:

- **Intake cap** (default 5 new people/day, configurable). Meeting 30 people at a wedding does not
  create 30 active items — extras are parked in a **Roster** and promoted at the cap rate,
  prioritised by likelihood of meeting again. Nothing is lost; the queue just never explodes.
- **Daily ceiling** (default 25 retrievals). Overflow is deferred, never dropped.
- **Value-first triage.** When over capacity, order by *who you are likely to see next*, not by
  due date. A colleague beats a stranger from a conference. This is the sharpest departure from
  generic SRS and it is only possible because the items are people.
- **Amnesty.** After an absence, the app offers to redistribute the whole backlog across the next
  fortnight — one tap, no guilt copy. Returning after two weeks away must never present a wall.
- **Quiet-week substitution.** Zero new people is a *normal week*. The streak is satisfiable by
  maintenance retrievals, a cast/place-track session, or a protocol rehearsal, so the habit does
  not require an extroverted social calendar.

## 5. What the streak actually counts

Not "opened the app." Not "met someone." **A day counts when you complete the day's due
retrievals or explicitly rest.** That definition is:
- satisfiable on a day with no social contact (loop 5 substitution),
- not satisfiable by mere app-opening (no hollow streaks),
- aligned with the mechanism that actually produces the outcome (retrieval, not exposure).

**Freezes:** 2 held, 1 earned per 7 adherent days, auto-applied. **Rest days:** 1/week, declared
in advance, does not consume a freeze. **Break recovery:** a broken streak shows the *lifetime*
retrieval count immediately beneath it, so the number that resets is never the biggest number
on screen.

## 6. Anti-patterns this app will not ship

A charter, enforced in code review:

1. No manufactured urgency, countdown timers, or "your friends are ahead."
2. No guilt copy. The empty state after a lapse reads *"Welcome back. Here's a lighter queue."*
3. No streak-loss dark patterns: no paid streak repair, no ads-for-freezes.
4. No vanity metrics that are not causally connected to remembering names.
5. No notification the user did not configure, and none between 22:00 and 07:00 except the
   opt-in pre-sleep review.
6. No infinite feed, no novelty content, no "daily quest" treadmill unrelated to names.
7. **No claim the evidence does not support** — this is an engagement decision, not just an
   ethical one. Over-promising produces the disappointment that ends the year at month three.

## 7. Instrumented engagement hypotheses

Because it would be embarrassing to ship an evidence-led memory app with a faith-based engagement
model, the loop is instrumented locally and testable by the user on their own data:

| # | Hypothesis | Local measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Pre-sleep slot is the strongest habit anchor | completion rate by time-of-day bucket |
| H2 | Field missions raise real-world recall more than extra drills | recall@1wk for mission-touched vs. untouched people |
| H3 | Intake capping prevents lapse | days-since-lapse vs. rolling intake volume |
| H4 | Moment Journal reviews predict next-week adherence | adherence after journal-review weeks vs. others |
| H5 | Streak length is *not* the driver; retrieval volume is | correlate both with recall@1mo |

H5 is deliberately adversarial toward the app's own headline mechanic. If the data says the
streak is decorative, the streak should go.
